**Coral — Investor Summary**

Coral is an early-stage startup targeting Canada first, with North American expansion as a longer-term opportunity. The founder reports no active users yet and seeks funding for hosting, APIs, email and the database. This brief draws on a review of the current project code; live pricing, revenue and investment terms have not been verified. Financial examples below are illustrations, not forecasts.

**The core proposition**

Coral is a hiring and talent platform for Canadian employers and job seekers, connecting talent discovery, recruitment management and career preparation. Employers can manage recruitment from job posting through screening, interviews and offers. Candidates can find opportunities, improve applications and practise interviews within the same platform.

The commercial proposition is to help employers spend less time coordinating recruitment and reviewing applications, while giving candidates a more useful path from preparation to employment. These benefits should be measured in customer pilots.

**The most important parts of the product**

- Employer hiring tools: job posting, applicant tracking, candidate search, outreach, interview booking, shared scorecards and offers.
- AI assistance: candidate matching and structured initial screening to support recruiter review.
- Candidate tools: profiles, job matching, application tracking, resume assistance and personalized interview practice.
- Employer branding and analytics: company profiles and visibility into recruitment activity.
- A connected workflow: employers and candidates can use the same system throughout the recruitment process.

The code includes frontend and backend implementations for these workflows. Their presence does not establish production reliability, commercial adoption or measured hiring improvements.

**Why customers could pay**

Employers are the strongest proposed initial paying customer because recruiting consumes staff time and operating budget. If Coral's subscription costs less than the time and recruitment spending it saves, a customer has an economic reason to buy and renew. Targeting employers that hire regularly could make recurring subscriptions more sustainable than targeting businesses with occasional vacancies.

Candidates can use free services, with an optional Premium subscription for additional career assistance. Candidate subscriptions offer another source of revenue, but their retention should be assessed separately because a successful job search may reduce demand.

**How Coral can earn money**

- Employer Pro subscriptions, billed monthly or annually.
- Negotiated Enterprise subscriptions for larger hiring teams.
- Optional job-seeker Premium subscriptions.

Employer Starter access is free. Job boosts are currently included in paid-plan allowances; they should not be presented as a separate proven revenue stream. Freelance marketplace commissions and other future services are expansion ideas, not current operating revenue.

Actual prices must be taken from the configured billing system. Old marketing prices and website testimonials are not evidence of current pricing or customer results.

**An illustration of revenue potential**

Assume an average employer subscription of CAD 100 per month. This is a model assumption, not confirmed Coral pricing. Excluding candidate subscriptions and any Enterprise price differences:

| Paying employers at that run rate | Monthly subscription revenue | Annualized subscription revenue |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 10 | CAD 1,000 | CAD 12,000 |
| 50 | CAD 5,000 | CAD 60,000 |
| 100 | CAD 10,000 | CAD 120,000 |
| 500 | CAD 50,000 | CAD 600,000 |

These figures describe steady subscription revenue run rates, not dated growth forecasts. They exclude discounts, refunds and taxes collected. They are not profit: AI services, hosting, payment fees, customer support, salaries and customer acquisition all consume revenue.

For the initial Canadian market, choose a customer segment and estimate reachable employers, then multiply realistic paying adoption by annual revenue per employer. A vacancy count is not a count of paying employers or a monetary estimate of Coral's market.

**How an investor could receive a return**

For an equity investment, the investor owns an agreed share of the company. Retained customers, growing recurring revenue and improving profitability can support a higher company value. A cash return could come through an acquisition, an approved sale of the investor's shares, or dividends if the company becomes profitable and declares distributions.

A higher valuation alone is not cash received, and company revenue is not automatically paid to investors.

For illustration, if an investor still owns 5% when a company sale leaves CAD 2 million available to shareholders, their proportional proceeds would be CAD 100,000 before tax. Whether that is a good return depends on the amount invested, the time elapsed, dilution, preferential share rights and other payout adjustments. This is not a proposed valuation, funding amount or forecast of Coral's exit. Returns depend on the actual investment agreement and business performance.

**The project's growth potential**

The opportunity is to become a recurring hiring service for a clearly defined Canadian customer segment, then expand into larger employers and other North American markets after proving adoption and retention. A proposed initial focus is small and medium-sized employers that hire regularly. Useful employer tools can support revenue even while the talent network is developing. As relevant candidates and hiring employers join, successful matches could make the network more valuable and support referrals.

Statistics Canada reported 510,200 job vacancies in the second quarter of 2026. That shows substantial hiring activity to investigate, but it does not establish demand for Coral or the number of potential paying customers. [Statistics Canada, released 15 September 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260915/dq260915a-eng.htm)

Durable differentiation will depend on a useful concentration of employers and candidates, customer relationships, trusted hiring workflows and measured results. AI functionality and a long feature list alone do not establish a competitive advantage.

**What the investment should achieve**

The requested investment will cover the infrastructure needed to launch and operate initial Canadian pilots:

| Cost category | Purpose | Budget basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hosting | Run the website and backend reliably | Monthly hosting cost and expected traffic |
| Database | Store accounts, profiles, jobs and applications | Monthly database, storage and backup costs |
| APIs | Provide AI matching, screening and career assistance | Expected usage per user and cost per request |
| Email | Send account, application and interview messages | Expected monthly email volume |

Funding request = setup costs + monthly operating budget × months of runway + a stated contingency. Confirm actual provider quotations and usage assumptions before inserting an amount. The budget should show both low-usage launch costs and how costs increase as users join.

Infrastructure funding should enable a measurable commercial test. Pair launch with founder-led customer recruitment and onboarding. Proposed first milestones are 5–10 Canadian employer pilots, the first paid conversions and evidence that employers return to use Coral. These are targets, not existing customers. Then track:

- Paying employers and reconciled monthly recurring revenue.
- Customer renewal and cancellation rates.
- Customer acquisition cost and time to recover it from gross profit.
- Gross margin after AI, hosting and direct support costs.
- Completed hiring workflows and measured recruiter time savings.
- AI screening reliability, fairness and hiring-outcome validation.

Set numeric targets and a runway budget using the requested investment amount and actual operating costs. Hosting alone does not generate revenue; customer adoption and renewal must follow.

**Facts to confirm before the meeting**

Confirm the first Canadian customer segment, launch date, actual prices, infrastructure budget, requested funding, proposed terms and runway. State the absence of active users clearly. Include customer interviews or pilot commitments when available; describe prospective customers separately from signed commitments and paying accounts.

Use reconciled billing records for revenue. Premium user counts can include complimentary access, and dashboard totals do not by themselves establish subscription receipts or recurring revenue. Do not present demonstration charts, hard-coded testimonials or marketing claims such as “85% faster hiring” as measured results.

The current screening documentation explicitly says the scorer has not been validated for hiring outcomes and has no completed independent bias audit. Present AI screening as support for human review, and make validation a pilot milestone. Internal reference: [scoring validation](C:/coral/coral/backend/docs/scoring-validation.md).
